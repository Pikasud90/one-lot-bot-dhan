## The book (one definition, used everywhere)
- Money: Rs 1,00,000 at the start, never topped up. Idle cash earns nothing (say so).
- A signal on the evening of day t asks for a place. Places: at most K trades active (waiting
  to fill, open, or waiting to exit) at that evening, counted as the product counts them
  (`src/onelot/delivery/wide.py`). A trade that gets a place keeps it until it is closed.
- A trade is the study's own trade: its entry date and price, exit date and price, share
  count and costs are taken from `trades_P1.pkl` unchanged. The cap only decides WHICH trades
  the book holds. A trade the book cannot pay for in cash is refused and counted.
- The book is marked to market every session on the stocks' daily closes.
- Check, before anything else: with K = 10 and the product's order your book must hold the
  same trades as the product's cap tier (2,560 on the lead's run). Explain every difference.

## What is compared (exactly these; fixed)
1. ORDER among the signals of one evening, K = 10:
   PRODUCT (desk 18 first, then the deepest pullback: the product's own measure of depth),
   DEEP (deepest first), SHALLOW (shallowest first), LIQUID (highest median rupee turnover of
   the last 20 sessions first), CALM (lowest standard deviation of the last 20 daily returns
   first), RANDOM (500 seeded shuffles: a distribution, the yardstick of luck). An order may
   use only what is known at the close of day t.
2. CAP, with the PRODUCT order: K = 5, 10, 20, and no cap (money: K x Rs 10,000, and for no
   cap the largest number ever open x Rs 10,000).
3. BENCHMARKS, like for like (no dividends in any leg; say so):
   B1 the same Rs 1 lakh in NIFTY, bought on the first day and held;
   B2 NIFTY with the SAME rupees invested as the book on each day (the book's exposure, its
      idle cash idle too);
   B3 the equal-weight basket of the 222 stocks with the same rupees invested as the book on
      each day. The 222 stocks were chosen in November 2026, so their own history is
      flattered by survival: B3 carries that same flattery, B1 and B2 do not. The rule's own
      contribution is the book less B3.

## What to report
For each order and each cap, and for TUNE / CHOOSE / TEST and the whole span:
- trades held, trades refused, winners, % a trade after costs with t clustered by entry week;
- the book: end value, yearly growth rate, the worst fall from a peak, the worst calendar
  year, the average number of places in use, the share of sessions with all places in use;
- the book less B1, less B2, less B3: in rupees and in % a year, with a 95% interval from a
  block bootstrap over calendar months (2,000 draws, seed fixed);
- for each fixed order: where it stands in the RANDOM distribution (percentile of % a trade
  and of the yearly growth rate).
Then the plain answers, each in two or three sentences with its numbers:
1. What did Rs 1 lakh become under the product's rule, and what did B1, B2 and B3 become?
2. Is the book ahead of B3 beyond chance? Ahead of B2? Say "not shown" where the interval
   holds zero.
3. Is any fixed order better than RANDOM? The rule, fixed now: better means beyond the 97.5th
   percentile of RANDOM in TUNE AND above its median in CHOOSE and in TEST. If none is, say
   that the product's order is as good as any and should stay.
4. Which cap? Fixed now: do not recommend a change unless a cap beats K = 10 on the book
   less B3 in all three periods.
5. What does the cap cost? Compare the trades the cap refused with the ones it took (% a
   trade, and on which kind of evening the book is full).
6. What should the owner expect from the live paper test: capped trades a month, and how
   many months until a result of the measured size could be told from zero at t = 2 (from
   the mean and the spread of the capped trades; give the figure for tier all as well).
7. What this cannot tell (survival of the 222 names, adjusted prices, fills at the open, no
   dividends, taxes, a start date chosen by the data).
