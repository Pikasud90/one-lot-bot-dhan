## The record (read-only, in the main checkout's `data/onelot.db`)
`desk_signals` (153 alerts, 2026-10-29 to 2026-11-23, `payload` JSON holds the whole alert),
`paper_trades` (one per alert: plan, fills, result, close reason) and `paper_events`,
`signal_evaluations`, `fill_quotes` (7,290 recorded option quotes around alerts),
`gate_audit`, `engine_events`, `market_snapshots`; one-minute bars in `bars`; real option
bars in `data/expired_options.db`. Read the code that writes them before trusting a column
(`src/onelot/desk/desk.py`, `src/onelot/paper/`, `src/onelot/desk/fill_costs.py` or where
`fill_quotes` is written). The strategy line-up changed on 2026-11-12 (before: several slots
such as supertrend_vol and supertrend_mtf; after: only `microtrend` on the indices and the
`pullback` delivery rule on stocks) and the stop, target and trail rule came on 2026-11-24.
PRIVATE, do not read or print rows of: `desk_positions`, `positions`, `trade_journal`,
`account_cache`, `app_settings` (the owner's own actions and account). Counts of taken and
skipped alerts from `desk_signals.status` are fine.

## Part A: what happened (facts, no opinion)
1. Inventory: alerts by day, instrument, slot (strategy and timeframe), side, status, and
   data grade (live Dhan bars or delayed Yahoo bars: `paper_trades.data_source` / `grade`);
   days on which the desk did not run or ran on delayed data.
2. Results of the paper trades as recorded: by slot, instrument, side, hour of entry, close
   reason; trades, % a trade and rupees a trade, winners %, payoff; t by entry day. Split at
   2026-11-12 (the line-up change).
3. For each alert, from the stored one-minute bars: the best and the worst move of the
   managed price before the exit (in % and in units of the planned risk), the time to the
   best point, how many reached 25%, 50%, 80% and 100% of the planned move, and how many of
   those still ended as a loss.
4. Fill costs for real: from `fill_quotes`, the spread of the alerted contracts (rupees and %
   of premium, by index and by hour) against what the cost model assumed; say what the model
   should assume if they differ.

## Part B: live against the backtest (is the desk doing what was tested?)
For the `microtrend` slot since 2026-11-12: run the product strategy on the STORED bars of
the same days and compare signal by signal with the live alerts (same bar, same side). List
alerts the backtest has and the desk missed (desk down, delayed bars), alerts the desk raised
that the backtest lacks, and the difference in result for the matched ones (entry price,
exit time, reason). `scripts/microtrend_parity.py` and `scripts/ratchet_desk_replay.py` show
how a replay is done. One table and one sentence: is the live desk a faithful copy?

## Part C: the same alerts under other rules ("how our experiments would have performed")
On the SAME live alerts (entry at the open of the minute after the alert unless stated), with
model and real premiums:
 1. as recorded;
 2. the desk's rule (ratchet: first stop 1.5 x ATR(14) of 60m bars, target 3 risks, extend);
 3. time exits after 15, 30 and 60 minutes;
 4. the opposite side (a fade of every alert);
 5. entry delayed by 1, 3 and 5 minutes (a person reading a Telegram message is late: what
    does each minute cost?);
 6. only alerts taken in the first hour, the middle of the day, the last 90 minutes;
 7. only alerts where the previous session was "active" (`src/onelot/mllab/states.py`), and
    only where India VIX rose in the previous session.
The sample is small (153 alerts, about 17 sessions): give every number with its trade count
and day count, and say plainly that nothing here can be proven on this sample alone.

## Part D: hypotheses from the live record, tested on five years
From A to C write down at most FIVE hypotheses a trader would form (for example: "alerts
before 09:45 lose", "alerts against the 60m trend lose", "the first alert of the day beats
later ones", "a 3 minute delay turns the result negative"). Test each on the five-year
history of the `microtrend` rule with the harness (TUNE / CHOOSE / TEST, the selection rule
of RESEARCH-RULES section 5; five hypotheses = five cells, plus their opposites as AVOID
candidates). A hypothesis that the history does not support is dropped, however good it
looked live. For one that passes, write the exact change to the rule it implies (a filter in
`config/desk.yaml` or in the strategy), but do NOT change product code: the lead decides.
