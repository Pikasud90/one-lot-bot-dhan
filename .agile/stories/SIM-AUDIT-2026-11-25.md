# Simulator audit of 25 November 2026 (before the rebuild)

Four agents, no code changed: one used the tab in a real browser on real bars (findings
SIM-01 to SIM-13), one measured the server on the real 15 GB store opened read-only (F1 to
F10), one mapped the code, one compared premium replay tools. This file is their output,
lightly shortened. Severity: blocker (breaks the use or the owner named it), major, minor.

## Using it (browser, real bars)

I audited the Simulator in a real browser on the private copy (port 8794), using real bars from 1
  Jul 2026 and real option premiums. I changed no code and committed nothing. Both of the owner's
  bugs are reproduced, measured and traced to their cause. BUG 1 (the price scale): after a switch
  from NIFTY to RELIANCE the price axis ran from -2,218 to 30,720. RELIANCE candles at 1,152 to
  1,281 got 2 px of a 430 px pane, and HDFCBANK candles at 669 to 701 got 0 px. The old NIFTY
  stop, entry and target lines (23,552, 23,698, 24,133) stayed drawn on the stock chart. They were
  still there three sessions later, back on NIFTY with no trade open. The same RELIANCE session on
  a freshly loaded page fills 70% of the pane, which confirms the cause. In sim.js, resetRun()
  empties S.lineKey but never clears S.scaleLevels or S.lines. For a new session with no alert,
  syncLines() computes the key "", finds it equal to the emptied key and returns early. So the
  autoscale provider (mergeRange) keeps the old levels and the old price lines are never removed.
  The fault comes back on every Start or Resume made while an alert or a trade was showing. BUG 2
  (the lag): changing the instrument dropdown itself takes 15 ms, so the browser is not what
  blocks. The wait comes after Start, in the request POST /api/sim/sessions. In the browser it
  took 48.4 s (first, cold), 15.3 s, 21.3 s and 9.0 s; over curl it took 5.7 to 40.8 s. The
  machine's load average was 24 to 53 on 8 cores, so these wall times are inflated. The real work
  is 1.6 to 2.3 s of server CPU on every start, never cached: building the same instrument a
  second time costs the same again. That work is SimSession.__init__ loading 150 days of one-
  minute bars (about 36,000), then running the whole strategy over all of them (build_signals),
  then the daily bars, the NIFTY bars for a stock, and the option pricer. While it runs, other
  requests slow down because the work holds Python's global lock: /api/state went from about 20 ms
  to a median of 44 to 172 ms and a maximum of 0.78 to 1.22 s, and /api/gates took 8.9 s once. On
  the owner's 15 GB store, available_resolutions() counts every stored bar of the instrument and
  takes 0.44 to 1.42 s per call. It runs 4 to 6 times per start, which should add roughly 2 to 8
  s, and 23 times on the first options load. Meanwhile the page shows nothing: the Start button
  only greys out, and the old chart, the old status and the old trade lines stay up for up to 48
  s. Starting a new session also silently closes the open trade of the old one. Beyond the two
  bugs the experience is far from a professional replay tool. At 1440x900 the chart starts 626 px
  down the page and only 274 of its 460 px are visible. The right column (420 px) is empty until
  an alert. Seeing the chart, controls, alert and log together needs about 800 px of scrolling. At
  1024x768 only 40 px of the chart is visible and the alert drops below the chart. On a phone the
  chart starts 1,454 px down. The chart has no volume, no strategy lines, no price readout under
  the cursor, and it fits 300 candles (2 to 5 days), so today is a thin slice. For an option the
  plan and the log show only index points, never the premium's stop and target. One log row reads
  In 23,668.55, Out 23,673.30, gross -Rs 159: the index rose but the trade lost, because the
  premium fell from 572.9 to 570.45. At 60x and 300x there is no in-between drawing and the page
  sends one request per replayed minute (4.7 a second). Play stops at the end of every trading
  day. Taking a trade restarts play at the old speed, 300x.

### SIM-01 (blocker): Old stop, entry and target levels squash the price scale and stay drawn after 
- Area: chart / price scale (owner bug 1)
- Evidence: Steps: 1440x900, Day theme. NIFTY, microtrend, 3m, start 2026-11-03 09:15, played at
  300x to the 13:12 alert, took it (trade open), then switched to RELIANCE, vwap_pullback, 5m and
  pressed Start. Scale read from the chart itself (setData array against priceToCoordinate and
  coordinateToPrice): axis -2,217.94 to 30,720.49, candles 1,152.5 to 1,281, candle height 2 px of
  a 430 px pane (0%). After 39 minutes of 300x play it was still -2,218 to 30,720 and 0% (candle
  high 1,307). Then HDFCBANK, same strategy: axis -2,772 to 30,859, candles 669.2 to 700.75, 0 px.
  Then back to NIFTY microtrend from 2026-11-05: candles fill 57%, but the stop 23,552.53, entry
  23,697.60 and target 24,132.80 of the trade from three sessions earlier are still drawn
  (screenshot), with no trade open. Control: a fresh page reload, then RELIANCE vwap_pullback 5m:
  axis 1,133.65 to 1,317.84, fill 70%. Same NIFTY session before the switch: 60% with no levels,
  39% with alert or trade levels, because the target 441 points away squeezes the candles.
- Cause: src/onelot/ui/sim.js resetRun() sets S.lineKey = "" but never resets S.scaleLevels or
  S.lines. syncLines() builds key = "" when there is no alert or trade, sees key === S.lineKey and
  returns before it reassigns S.scaleLevels and before it removes the old price lines. The series'
  autoscaleInfoProvider (mergeRange(orig(), S.scaleLevels)) keeps widening the range to the old
  instrument's levels. drawAll() calls setData and fitContent but never resets the price scale.
  startSessionNow() also ignores the /end answer of the old session, so its CLOSED state is never
  applied. The same path runs on Resume, on a timeframe or strategy change (any Start), and after
  End. Not the cause: price format (precision 2 is fine for stocks), the candle merge, or old
  candles kept (setData replaces them).
- Fix proposed: Make a session switch a hard reset of the chart. Either remove the candlestick
  series (or the whole chart) and create a new one per session, or, in resetRun, set S.scaleLevels
  = null, remove every line in S.lines, set S.lineKey = null (a sentinel that never equals a
  computed key), clear the markers and call priceScale('right').applyOptions({autoScale:true})
  after setData. Show only the active trade's levels. Set autoscale margins (scaleMargins top 0.1,
  bottom 0.1). Let the stop and target widen the range only when they lie within a few ATR of
  price; otherwise show them as off-screen axis labels, as TradingView does, instead of squashing
  the candles. Add a test: start NIFTY with an alert, then start RELIANCE, and assert the visible
  range lies within 20% of the candles' range and that no price lines remain.

### SIM-02 (blocker): Every Start rebuilds 150 days of bars and the whole strategy from scratch: 5.7
- Area: server / session start (owner bug 2)
- Evidence: Changing the instrument dropdown: 15.5 ms of page work (renderSetup only). Start
  triggers POST /api/sim/sessions/{old}/end (87 to 195 ms), then POST /api/sim/sessions. Browser
  timings: 48,424 ms (first start, cold), 15,282 ms (NIFTY to RELIANCE), 21,298 ms (to HDFCBANK),
  8,992 ms (back to NIFTY), 13,266 ms (RELIANCE on a fresh page). Curl timings: 28.3, 27.2, 40.8,
  32.5, 9.8, 12.7, 7.4, 9.5, 12.6 and 5.7 s. Load average 24 to 53 on 8 cores during these runs.
  In-process profile (SimManager.create): NIFTY microtrend 3m, wall 26.4 s, CPU 2.32 s; RELIANCE
  vwap_pullback 5m, CPU 2.24 s; HDFCBANK, CPU 2.21 s; NIFTY orb 5m, CPU 1.63 s; RELIANCE again,
  CPU 2.34 s (no cache benefit); NIFTY microtrend again, CPU 2.07 s. 36,445 (index) or 35,275
  (stock) one-minute bars loaded per start. Breakdown, NIFTY microtrend: build_signals 19.4 of
  26.4 s wall (strategy generate 15.2 s, microtrend features 14.6 s; pandas tz_localize and
  normalize 8.6 s in session_key and bar_of_day), StoreBars.minute_bars 6.1 s (_read_bars 5.1 s),
  daily_bars 3.6 s, resample 2.8 s. Page side: one long task of 54 to 85 ms, largest gap in a 50
  ms interval probe 87 to 119 ms, so the browser itself is not blocked. Once a session exists, the
  cost is small: advance(1) 2.4 to 7.1 ms, advance(300) 6 to 341 ms, GET state 1.9 to 64 ms.
- Cause: src/onelot/sim/engine.py SimSession.__init__ calls _load(start + HORIZON_DAYS) with
  HISTORY_DAYS = 120, so 150 calendar days of one-minute bars. _build_frame then runs
  build_signals over all of them: the full strategy, consistent_daily, the market index's minutes
  and daily bars for a stock, the 60m ATR resample, and _pricer (INDIAVIX daily bars,
  StoredPremiums on expired_options.db). Nothing is reused between sessions: StoreBars caches only
  raw minutes, and the strategy frame is rebuilt on every create. routes.py runs create inline in
  a sync handler, so the user waits for all of it.
- Fix proposed: (1) Cache per instrument, process-wide, with an LRU: the cleaned minute frame, the
  daily bars, and the strategy frame keyed by (instrument, strategy, timeframe, params, data
  version). A second start on the same instrument, or a start on another date inside the cached
  range, should then take under 100 ms. (2) Return the candles at once: one cheap query for the
  last 300 candles. Build the signal frame in a background worker and report readiness (create
  returns status LOADING; the page polls or the first advance waits on it). (3) Load only the
  warm-up each strategy needs (a declared warm-up in bars per timeframe) instead of a fixed 120
  days. (4) Vectorise the timezone work in indicators/core.py session_key and bar_of_day: one
  integer offset in place of tz_localize and normalize. Target: switching instrument shows the new
  chart in under 300 ms and playable signals in under 1 s on the owner's machine.

### SIM-03 (blocker): A session start slows every other request of the desk (Python global lock and 
- Area: server / whole app
- Evidence: Idle /api/state: 505 (first), 39, 23, 8 and 21 ms. Polled every 200 ms during each of
  6 starts: median 88, 157, 115, 172, 62 and 44 ms; maximum 893, 1,221, 1,043, 1,201, 1,100 and
  784 ms. /api/gates?hours=24 took 8,925 ms during the first start and 82 to 1,835 ms otherwise.
  One POST /take took 9,977 ms under load (294 ms when quiet). The owner's live desk runs in the
  same process, so its state, alerts and stream share the stall.
- Cause: src/onelot/sim/routes.py: sim_create is a sync def that runs SimManager.create in
  FastAPI's threadpool, inside the desk's own process. The pandas and numpy work in SIM-02 is CPU-
  bound and holds the global lock for long stretches, so every other request handler of the desk
  waits for it. One manager RLock (m.lock) also serialises every simulator action across all
  sessions.
- Fix proposed: Run the build of a simulator session in a separate process (a ProcessPoolExecutor
  with one or two workers, or a small simulator worker) so the desk's process never does the heavy
  work. Keep the per-session state in the worker, or send the compact numpy arrays back. Use one
  lock per session, not one per manager. Add a guard test: during a create, /api/state stays under
  100 ms at the 95th percentile.

### SIM-04 (major): Requests that grow with the size of the store: available_resolutions counts ever
- Area: server / store size
- Evidence: Owner's 15 GB store, opened read-only with SQLite: the SELECT resolution, min(ts),
  max(ts), count(*) ... GROUP BY resolution behind available_resolutions took 0.438 s for NIFTY
  (1m: 468,601 rows from 2021-11-03; also 1d, 2m, 5m, 15m and 60m) and 1.415 s for RELIANCE
  (467,643 one-minute rows). The private store (255,660 rows in all) answers in milliseconds. GET
  /api/sim/options took 0.016 to 0.32 s here; it covers 23 instruments, so on the owner's store an
  estimated 10 to 30 s on the first visit, and again every 300 s (OPTIONS_TTL_S). Each create
  calls it in _range (minute_range), in load_bars once per minute_bars call (the instrument, plus
  NIFTY for a stock), in daily_bars, and in _more_past_data. That is an estimated 2 to 8 s added
  per start on the owner's store. The 150-day _read_bars itself grows only with the depth of
  history read, not with the total size of the store.
- Cause: src/onelot/data/store.py available_resolutions() (COUNT(*) per resolution over the
  covering index), called from sim/bars.py StoreBars.minute_range, daily_bars and load_bars, and
  from SimManager.options(), _range() and SimSession._more_past_data().
- Fix proposed: Keep first and last per (series, resolution) in a small metadata table, or an in-
  memory cache filled once and refreshed when bars are written. Drop COUNT(*): MIN and MAX on the
  (series_key, resolution, ts) index are near-instant. Let load_bars take a known resolution so it
  skips the discovery. Serve /api/sim/options from that cache.

### SIM-05 (major): StoreBars cache grows with every session and makes the daily-bar rebuild slower 
- Area: server / memory
- Evidence: StoreBars.minute_bars joins every frame it reads into self._minutes[series]
  (join_bars(seen, df)), and nothing is ever evicted. daily_bars keys its cache on len(minutes),
  so each new start on another date changes n, misses the cache and reruns consistent_daily over
  the whole accumulated frame (2.28 s for 3 calls in the RELIANCE profile, under load). Over a day
  of use the process holds an ever larger frame for every instrument touched.
- Cause: src/onelot/sim/bars.py StoreBars.minute_bars and StoreBars.daily_bars.
- Fix proposed: A bounded per-series frame covering the union of the ranges asked for, with LRU
  eviction across series, and daily bars computed once per series and data version (see SIM-02).

### SIM-10 (major): Playback does not feel like a replay: one round trip per minute, no drawing in b
- Area: playback
- Evidence: 10x: 15.0 s of wall time advanced 2 minutes (2.5 expected, −20%); each minute waits
  for its advance request (507 and 368 ms under load). 60x: 15 minutes in 15.0 s, 14 requests (26
  to 185 ms); the chart jumps once a second. 300x: 71 minutes in 15.03 s (75 expected, −5%), 71
  requests (4.7 a second, average 42 ms, maximum 222 ms), no long tasks; a full trading day takes
  about 75 s, then play stops ('The trading day is finished. Press Play…') and the user must press
  Play again every day. 60x on return from another tab: 5 minutes in 6 s. After Take on an alert
  hit at 300x, play resumed at 300x by itself: 16 minutes passed within 4 s of the entry. Close
  while paused waits for Play or Step, and Step then jumps a whole 3-minute candle. No step back,
  no jump to a date or time, no 'next alert' or 'skip to the next day' control.
- Cause: src/onelot/ui/sim.js tick(): startMinute() for speeds up to 10 (one request per minute
  before it animates); minutesToAsk returns 1 per 60 owed seconds above 10x, so one request per
  minute; absorb() sets S.playing = false on day_end; actNow() calls setPlaying(true) after take
  or skip when paused by the alert; the setInterval runs every 100 ms, not on animation frames.
- Fix proposed: Ask the server for chunks of minutes with their events (still never past the
  cursor the user has reached) and play them out locally on requestAnimationFrame at the chosen
  speed, with intra-candle drawing at every speed. Show events at their own minute. Keep the next
  chunk requested ahead so the clock never waits. Add an option to go on across days. After a
  take, stay paused, or drop to 1x until the fill, then let the user choose. Add 'jump to next
  alert', 'jump to time', and a candle-close countdown.

## Speed (server, real store)

The owner's "changing the instruments lags the whole app for a few seconds" has two server-side
  causes, both measured on the real 15 GB store opened read-only. 1. Every pandas date operation
  on the app's IST time zone (zoneinfo.ZoneInfo, src/onelot/clock.py:20) runs element by element
  and holds Python's global interpreter lock (GIL). One DatetimeIndex.normalize() on 36,000
  minutes took 2.35 s with ZoneInfo, 0.006 s with the string 'Asia/Kolkata' and 0.001 s with a
  fixed +05:30 offset. While it ran, a 10 ms ticker thread was frozen for 2.1 s. During a real
  session create, the ticker saw freezes of up to 0.68 s, about 4.5 s in all per create. The desk
  loop, the feed, the chain poller and the API event loop share that one process, so the whole app
  stops. 2. A session create takes 6 to 48 s, because nothing is cached and the store is queried
  wastefully. available_resolutions() runs min, max and COUNT(*) over every bar of a series, about
  470,000 index entries and 2.3 to 4.3 s each even from the sqlite3 command line. A create calls
  it 3 or 4 times. It also reads 150 calendar days of one-minute bars for the instrument plus the
  market index, about 72,000 to 77,000 rows, through slow SQLAlchemy row objects and DataFrame
  building. It then builds the strategy's signals over all 150 days (2,400 to 12,500 candles) and
  recomputes the daily bars of 3 series. GET /api/sim/options on the live desk took 20.1 s cold
  and 4 to 15 ms while its 300 s memo held. It runs the COUNT query for 23 instruments, and the
  min and max dates alone take 0.04 s per series with an index seek. Changing instrument, strategy
  or timeframe in the page sends no request. The wait comes when the owner presses Start: an
  awaited POST /end, then an awaited POST /api/sim/sessions that took 6 to 48 s here. After a
  session exists the server is fast. advance(1) has a median of 0.5 ms (p95 12.6 ms, maximum 178
  ms), advance(390) takes 6 to 96 ms, and the answers are about 0.4 KB for one minute and about 18
  to 20 KB for 390 minutes. 300x play sends one 1-minute advance every 200 ms (5 requests a
  second), which the server sustains easily. The "does not fit to scale for index and stock" bug
  is in the page, not the server. resetRun() does not clear S.scaleLevels or the old price lines.
  syncLines() then returns early because both line keys are empty. The autoscale keeps the
  previous instrument's entry, stop and target (about 25,000 for NIFTY) next to stock candles
  (about 1,400), so the new chart is squashed.

### F1 (blocker): ZoneInfo IST makes pandas date operations hold the GIL for seconds and freezes the
- Area: server: whole-process stall (GIL)
- Evidence: Microbenchmark on 36,000 one-minute IST stamps: normalize() took 2.35 s with
  ZoneInfo('Asia/Kolkata'), 0.006 s with the string 'Asia/Kolkata' and 0.001 s with a fixed +05:30
  offset. A 10 ms ticker thread's longest gap during that one normalize() was 2.1 s. During
  m.create(), the ticker's longest gaps were 0.62/0.59/0.57 s (RELIANCE vwap_pullback 5m) and

- Cause: src/onelot/clock.py:20 sets IST = ZoneInfo('Asia/Kolkata'). The sim's frames are tz-aware

### F9 (minor): 300x is 5 one-minute requests a second; ticks are dropped while a request is in flig
- Area: page: 300x play

## What premium replay tools do

Audit only, no code changed. Premium replay tools share one feel. The chart owns about 75 to 85
  percent of the window and is never resized by the controls. Replay controls are one slim
  floating or docked bar (play/pause, step, speed, jump). Time only moves forward through one
  clock, so every readout stays consistent. Position, stop and target are drawn on the chart and
  can be dragged. Changing symbol or timeframe is instant and keeps the replay clock. The two most
  common complaints are lag and clutter, which is exactly the owner's complaint. For One Lot Desk
  the cure is: (1) one shared replay clock and one chart that is built once, with series reused
  and data swapped (setData), never destroyed and recreated; (2) load the new instrument's bars
  off the main thread of attention (show a skeleton, cancel the stale request, cache bars per
  instrument/timeframe); (3) after every symbol or timeframe change, refit the price scale to the
  visible bars and re-pad it, because index prices (around 24,000 to 55,000) and stock prices
  (hundreds to a few thousand) differ by 100x and a stale autoscale range is the 'scale
  difference' bug; (4) a TradingView-style single control strip, a forming candle updated via
  series.update, a countdown to candle close, and entry/stop/target drawn as price lines plus a
  shaded risk/reward primitive; (5) keyboard-first control. Sources were official help pages for
  TradingView, MetaTrader 5, NinjaTrader and Lightweight Charts, plus third-party reviews
  (Trustpilot, blogs, forums). Items marked (unverified) come from my own knowledge or a single
  non-official source. I could not fetch Sierra Chart or thinkorswim OnDemand pages in this audit,
  so those two rows rely on my knowledge. PER-TOOL NOTES TradingView Bar Replay (official help
  verified). Layout: the replay bar is a small panel at the top of the chart; the chart keeps
  nearly the whole window; trading panel docks at the bottom and the watchlist or order tab on the
  right are optional. Controls: play/pause, step forward one bar, a speed menu (a set of fixed
  steps, adjustable during play), 'Jump to real-time', start point chosen by hovering (a vertical
  line follows the mouse, click to set), or by date, or first available bar, or random bar; the
  start can be changed mid-replay. Forming candle: the current candle grows tick by tick or bar by
  bar in the selected speed, the price axis label updates with it. Timeframe change: allowed
  during replay; larger frames wait until enough smaller bars exist; if the lower frame has no
  data at that moment it shows a 'data point unavailable' message and keeps the old one. Symbol
  change: replay position is kept; multi-chart layouts can replay in sync. Orders: paper trading
  orders in the replay panel fill on replay prices; position lines show entry with live P&L, stop
  and target are lines you drag; the long/short position drawing tool shows shaded green reward

  Complaints: intraday history is limited per symbol, some chart types are unsupported, no sub-bar

  spread, volume and indicator values at the cursor (verified). Readouts: equity and balance

  side so no offline use; multi-timeframe views; trading panel with long/short, stop and target

  Strength: it is the full platform, not a toy. Complaint: heavy, slow to load a day, controls
  buried in a menu. COMMON PATTERN: all of them separate three things the owner's tab mixes up:
  (a) the clock (play, speed, jump), (b) the chart (one object, never rebuilt), and (c) the book
  (position, orders, trades, statistics). The best ones keep (a) in one bar, (b) as the dominant
  area and (c) in a dock that can be collapsed.

### Must
- One persistent chart, data swapped on change: Create the chart, the candle series, the volume
  series and the overlay primitives once when the tab first opens. On instrument or timeframe
  change call setData on the same series and then refit; never call chart.remove() or rebuild the
  DOM. WHY: Rebuilding is the likeliest cause of the several-second app lag when changing
  instruments. Reuse makes the switch feel instant, which is the single biggest sign of a premium
  app. HOW: Keep one chart object; candleSeries.setData(bars); chart.timeScale().fitContent() or
  setVisibleLogicalRange; remove only price lines and detach per-trade primitives. Cache bars per
  (symbol, timeframe) in a Map so a switch back is instant. Profile the lag first (Performance
  tab) to confirm whether it is DOM rebuild, a large JSON parse, a synchronous server call, or a
  SQLite query on the 15 GB store.
- Fast, cancellable bar loading with a skeleton: On instrument change, immediately show the new
  name and a light skeleton on the chart, fetch only the bars needed (window around the replay
  cursor, for example 300 to 1000 bars, not the whole history), cancel the previous request with
  AbortController, and ignore stale responses by a request id. WHY: Users forgive 300 ms with
  feedback; they never forgive a frozen app. Stale responses arriving late are what make a UI feel
  jumpy. HOW: fetch with AbortController; server returns a bounded slice with an index on
  (instrument, ts); optionally return bars as column arrays, not row objects, to cut parse time;
  prefetch the chosen instrument's bars on hover or on selection of the dropdown; run heavy server
  work off the event loop (thread pool) so other tabs and the alerts feed do not stall.
- Instrument-aware price scale refit (the 'scale difference' fix): After every symbol, timeframe
  or start-point change, reset the price scale: turn autoscale on, clear any manual range, fit the
  visible bars, then add margins. Choose price precision and minMove per instrument (index 2
  decimals, stock 2 decimals, option premium 2 decimals with tick 0.05), and keep
  entry/stop/target lines inside the autoscale range. WHY: An index near 25,000 and a stock near
  800 differ 30x; a stale range leaves the candles squashed or off screen. Premium tools always
  land with the price centred. HOW: priceScale('right').applyOptions({autoScale:true,
  scaleMargins:{top:0.08,bottom:0.2}});
  series.applyOptions({priceFormat:{type:'price',precision:2,minMove:0.05}});
  chart.timeScale().fitContent() then scrollToRealTime or
  setVisibleLogicalRange({from:n-120,to:n+8}); add an autoscaleInfoProvider on the series that
  merges the trade levels (entry, stop, target) into the range so they never fall off screen; give
  a 'Fit' button and the key A for auto-fit.
- Responsive chart sizing that owns the window: The chart fills all space between the control
  strip and the bottom dock. Use a ResizeObserver on its container (not window resize) and call
  chart.applyOptions({width,height}) or autoSize:true. Never hard-code pixel heights. WHY: The
  'window does not fit to scale' complaint. Pro tools give the chart 75 to 85 percent of the
  window and it follows every layout change smoothly. HOW: createChart(el,{autoSize:true});
  container is a CSS grid with rows 'auto 1fr auto' and min-height:0 on the chart cell (the usual
  flexbox gotcha); the dock collapses to a one-line summary; on resize debounce nothing,
  requestAnimationFrame only.
- Single replay control strip: One slim bar above the chart: instrument picker, strategy picker,
  timeframe buttons, then play/pause, step one bar, speed (1x 2x 5x 10x plus 30x, 60x), a start-
  date picker, 'Go live' equivalent called 'Jump to latest', and a state badge (Paused, Playing at
  5x, Waiting for alert). WHY: Matches TradingView's replay bar; users learn it in seconds and it
  never shifts the chart. HOW: Plain DOM buttons with large type (owner wants LARGE, bright);
  speeds as a segmented control; speed keeps its value after pause (NinjaTrader's reset to 1x is a
  known annoyance); disable instead of hide unavailable controls so the layout does not jump.
- Smooth forming candle: Show the current candle growing while the bar plays (open fixed,
  high/low/close moving), then lock it at close. At fast speeds update the forming candle at most
  once per animation frame. WHY: The forming candle is what makes replay feel alive; stutter here
  reads as low quality. HOW: series.update({time,open,high,low,close}) for the same time replaces
  the last bar; interpolate sub-bar steps from the stored 1-minute bars (for 5m and above, step
  through the constituent 1m bars); drive playback from requestAnimationFrame with a time
  accumulator, not a setInterval per bar; batch several bars per frame at 10x and above and call
  update once per bar only if needed, or setData for the tail.
- One shared replay clock and timeframe changes without resetting: A single clock object holds the
  current replay time. Changing timeframe re-aggregates the same stored one-minute bars up to the
  clock and keeps the position; the forming candle of the larger frame is partial. WHY:
  TradingView keeps the moment when the frame changes; losing the place is the worst replay bug.
  HOW: Aggregate 1m to 5m, 15m, 60m on the client from one array so no server call is needed;
  setData(aggregated up to clock); keep the visible logical range centred on the cursor; do not
  allow a timeframe below the data resolution (disable the button, no error).
- Alert toast with Take / Skip and a pause option: When the strategy raises an alert, show a large
  on-page card with side, price, stop, target, a small risk summary, a countdown (optional) and
  the buttons Take (T) and Skip (S). Option to auto-pause on alert. A coloured marker appears on
  the signal bar. WHY: This is the heart of the owner's wish. A clear, large, non-blocking card is
  what makes it feel like a real desk. HOW: DOM card anchored top right of the chart, not a modal;
  series markers via createSeriesMarkers(series, markers) with arrowUp/arrowDown shapes and text;
  play a short sound (optional, off by default); at 10x and above auto-pause is on by default so
  the user is not overrun.
- Entry, stop and target drawn on the chart: After Take show three lines: entry (neutral), stop
  (red), target (green), each with a price tag and a label (Stop, Target, plus rupees at risk and
  reward). A shaded risk zone (entry to stop) and reward zone (entry to target) sit behind the
  candles. As the ratchet moves the stop (to breakeven at 25 percent, 35 percent locked at 50
  percent) and moves the target out from 80 percent, the lines slide and a small tag explains why
  ('Stop to breakeven'). WHY: TradingView's long/short position tool is the most imitated element
  in the category; the shaded zones make risk legible at a glance. HOW:
  series.createPriceLine({price,color,lineWidth:2,lineStyle,axisLabelVisible:true,title}) for each
  line, and applyOptions on the same line to move it (do not remove and recreate); the shaded
  zones as a series primitive (ISeriesPrimitive with a pane view whose renderer fills a rectangle
  between priceToCoordinate(entry) and priceToCoordinate(stop) from the entry bar's x to the right
  edge, with alpha about 0.12); markers for entry and exit; remove all on close. Keep colours
  colour-blind safe with a text label, not colour alone.
- Live trade card with P&L and ratchet state: A compact card (docked or floating over the chart)
  with side, quantity (one lot), entry, current price, open P&L in rupees and points, R-multiple,
  distance to stop and target, the ratchet stage, time in trade, and a Close now button. WHY: The
  position readout is what people look at 90 percent of the time while a replay runs. HOW: DOM
  card updated on each frame with textContent only; P&L colour (green or red) plus sign; a thin
  progress bar from stop to target with the price marker; the ratchet stages drawn as small ticks
  at 25, 50 and 80 percent.
- Close signal and exit markers: A distinct close notice when the rule closes the trade (stop,
  target, trail, strategy exit, end of day) with the reason and the result, plus an exit marker on
  the chart. WHY: The owner asked for 'a close signal'; reason text builds trust in the rules.
  HOW: createSeriesMarkers with a circle or square shape and text like 'Target +Rs 2,340'; toast
  with the same text; the trade row gets the reason code.
- Speed that never lies: Show the true achieved speed, keep the speed after pause, offer 1x, 2x,
  5x, 10x, 30x and Max, and drop to a lower frame rate rather than skipping events: an alert, a
  stop move or an exit must always be seen even at Max. WHY: Speed claims that skip events destroy
  trust; MT5 and NinjaTrader users complain about both slowness and overshoot. HOW: Process bars
  in the logic step but render once per frame; events (alert, stop move, exit) force a render and,
  at 10x and above, an automatic slow-down or pause for the alert; the engine step loop on the
  server or client is deterministic so the same start and speed give the same result.
- Instant, calm theme and typography: Bright Day theme by default, large type (16 to 18 px body,
  20 px for the trade card numbers), tabular figures, Night toggle that also re-themes the chart
  (grid, text, candle colours). WHY: The owner's preference; consistent contrast avoids the
  clutter pros complain about. HOW: chart.applyOptions({layout:{background,textColor},grid}) on
  theme change, series.applyOptions for candle colours; font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums; limit
  visible items on the chart to candles, volume, trade lines and markers.

### Should
- Click-the-chart to choose the start: A 'Pick start' mode: a vertical line follows the mouse,

  param.time; send the time to the server; confirm only if a trade is open (jumping back must
  close or warn, since NinjaTrader wipes history on a jump).
- Countdown to candle close and OHLC legend: A legend top left (instrument, timeframe, O H L C,

  line); the chart takes the full width and about 55 percent of the height; the dock is collapsed
  to one summary line by default and opens as an overlay sheet from the bottom; control strip

### Pitfalls
- Destroying and recreating the chart (or the whole tab DOM) on instrument change. This is the
  most likely cause of the multi-second lag; reuse the chart and swap data.
- Leaving the price scale in its old range after a symbol change. Index and stock prices differ
  30x to 100x, so candles look flat or vanish. Re-enable autoscale, refit, and include trade
  levels in the autoscale range.
- Using window resize instead of a ResizeObserver, or fixed pixel heights, or a flex child without
  min-height 0; the chart then overflows or does not fill its box.
- Fetching the whole history for one instrument, or running a blocking SQLite or Python call on
  the event loop of a server that also serves the alerts feed; one slow query then freezes the
  entire app. Fetch a bounded window, run it in a worker thread, and cache.
- Letting an old response overwrite a new selection (race when the user clicks quickly through
  instruments). Use AbortController and a request id.
- setInterval per bar for playback; timers drift and queue up at high speed. Use
  requestAnimationFrame with an accumulator, batch bars per frame.
- Calling setData on every step (full redraw) instead of series.update for the forming and the new
  bar.
- Removing and recreating price lines every frame; update the existing line with applyOptions.
  Also detach primitives on close to avoid leaks.
- Skipping events at high speed (an alert or exit passes unseen). Events must force a render and
  pause or slow down.
- Resetting speed to 1x after pause (a known NinjaTrader annoyance) or losing the replay position
  when the timeframe changes.
- Allowing a jump back with a trade open without a warning; NinjaTrader wipes the replay account
  history on a slide. Decide a clear rule and say it in plain words.
- Clutter: too many lines, labels, indicators and panels over the chart; keep to candles, volume,
  trade lines, markers and one legend. Use text labels as well as colour.
- Lookahead bias: the chart, the aggregated higher-timeframe bars and any indicator must only use
  bars up to the replay clock; the forming larger candle must be partial. Also the stored one-
  minute bars must not leak the future through the price-scale range (autoscale must be computed
  from visible, already-played bars).
- Small type, dark default theme, and jargon; the owner wants bright, large type and plain words.
- Relying on the live desk's data store in tests or experiments; use the worktree's fakes
  (sim_fake.js) and never touch port 8787 or the 15 GB store.
- Claiming market accuracy that one-minute bars cannot give: intrabar order of stop and target
  hits is ambiguous; state the rule (for example stop first when both are touched in the same
  minute) on the trade detail.

### What a change of instrument does
CHANGING A SELECT (instrument, strategy, timeframe, mode or start): the delegated change listener
  on #sim-root (sim.js:624-634) calls readSetup() (copies all five DOM values into S.setup). For
  the instrument it sets S.setup.start = '' and then calls renderSetup(). renderSetup (288-316)
  rewrites innerHTML of #sim-mode, #sim-inst, #sim-strat, #sim-tf, re-picks the strategy and
  timeframe that fit, sets min/max/value on #sim-start (default start = last stored day 09:15,
  clamped), toggles #sim-go.disabled, rewrites #sim-note, and writes S.setup to localStorage. It
  makes no request and does NOT touch the running session, chart, price lines, markers, scale, log
  or cards. So after changing the instrument the old instrument's chart and trade panel stay live
  under the new selection until Start. If a session is playing it keeps playing. PRESSING START:
  startSession (352) is guarded by S.starting. It disables #sim-go and calls startSessionNow
  (357): 1. readSetup again. 2. If a session is running, `await POST /end` on the old session.

### Work per advance
REQUESTS: one POST /advance per advance. At 1x to 10x the page asks for ONE minute per minute-
  animation: startMinute() posts advance(1) when no animation is running, animates it for 60/speed
  seconds (6 s at 10x, 60 s at 1x), then finishMinute(). So at 10x there is a round trip about
  every 6 seconds. At 60x and 300x it accrues owed time and posts advance(n) with n =
  floor(owed/60) up to 390 (runFast). In live mode, runFast(1) every 5 s. S.busy blocks
  overlapping advances. A stale response for another session id is dropped. There is no timeout or
  abort on any fetch. PER 100 ms TICK (setInterval, L664) while playing at speed <= 10x: anim.f +=
  dt/(60/speed s). Then drawLast(mergeBar(base, partialMinute(bar, f), tfm)) which is one
  series.update() for the forming candle every 100 ms, plus renderClock() (textContent). So 10
  updates a second at any slow speed. PER FINISHED MINUTE (finishMinute -> pushMinutes([bar]) ->
  absorb(res)): - mergeBar creates a new array; S.candles is mutated or pushed (grows for the
  whole session). - series.update(c). - healCandle(): if S.healT is set and the candle is later
  than healT, one GET /sessions/{id} (which makes the server build candles_view of 300 candles). -
  absorb: cursor, alert, trade, summary; each event is unshifted to S.events (capped to 60);
  toasts, beep and a pause on ALERT; EXIT_SIGNAL; on CLOSED a refresh() flag; on day_end it sets
  needRefresh; on over it sets playing false. - syncLines() (a string key compare; if changed it
  removes and recreates all price lines and sets autoScale). - syncMarkers() rebuilds the full
  markers array from alertMarks (unbounded) and all trades and calls setMarkers (full replace). -

### Structural problems
- Stale price lines and scale levels survive a new session (the 'does not fit to scale' bug)
  (src/onelot/ui/sim.js:236-243 and :324-330 (resetRun sets lineKey=''), :222
  (autoscaleInfoProvider uses S.scaleLevels)): syncLines returns early at L240 when key===lineKey,
  before it clears S.scaleLevels or removes S.lines. After switching from NIFTY (about 25,000)
  with an alert or trade showing to RELIANCE (about 1,400), the old levels stay in the price range
  and the candles are squeezed flat. Chart objects, lines, levels and markers are not owned by a
  session, so the next session inherits them.
- Changing instrument/strategy stalls the app 11-35 s with no feedback: session create does the
  full strategy build inline (src/onelot/sim/engine.py:206-310 (__init__, _load, _build_frame,
  build_signals L1281); src/onelot/sim/routes.py:70-74; src/onelot/ui/sim.js:352-377): Measured on
  the worktree's 50 MB store: NIFTY microtrend 3m create 11.7 s and 17.1 s unprofiled (35 s under
  cProfile, with 8 s loading bars, 19 s in microtrend features, 7.5 s resampling). It runs 150
  days of one-minute bars through the multi-timeframe strategy in a sync route in the live desk's
  own process. There is no loading state, no timeout or abort, and the old session is ended first
  (an extra call, no confirm). The select change itself is cheap. The same full rebuild also hits
  GET session (resume offer on tab open after restart: sim.js:577) and every 30 simulated days
  inside advance (engine.py:355-367), under the manager's one lock.
- No price format or tick size per instrument, no scale margins (src/onelot/ui/sim.js:221
  (addSeries without priceFormat); :204-211): Index (about 80,000 for SENSEX), stock (about 100 to
  3,000) and premium-priced options share one default 2-decimal format with no minMove. Scale
  margins are the default, so the stop, target and candles press against the edges. There is no
  per-instrument configuration object to carry this.
- Whole side panel, summary, log and events are rebuilt with innerHTML on every absorbed minute
  (src/onelot/ui/sim.js:538-555 (render), :522-537 (renderLog), :517-520 (renderSpeeds),
  :550-552): Every minute (every 6 s at 10x) the alert card, position card, tiles, the entire
  closed-trades table and the events list are recreated. This drops focus and hover, replaces a
  Take/Skip button under the pointer (a click can miss), removes the flash animation, and costs
  more as the log grows. There is no keyed or incremental update. Speed chips are rebuilt on each
  render, so keyboard focus on a speed chip is lost.
- Markers are rebuilt in full every render and grow without bound (src/onelot/ui/sim.js:257-269
  and :405 (alertMarks.push)): setMarkers with all alert, entry and exit markers is called on
  every render, even when nothing changed. alertMarks is never trimmed. A 20-day session with many
  alerts redraws hundreds of markers every minute.
- State is spread over S, the DOM and localStorage, and the chart has no owner
  (src/onelot/ui/sim.js:179-186, :317-321, :288-316): The select values are the source of truth
  that readSetup copies into S.setup; the cards are the only place the alert and trade are
  visible; chart, series, marks, lines, lineKey and scaleLevels live in S next to clock state with
  no session object that dies with the session. There is no separation of chart controller, panel
  view and engine client, so the bug above could not be fixed in one place.
- Chart is a fixed 460px/340px card and the page is a stack, not a viewport-filling workspace
  (src/onelot/ui/sim.css:13-18, :55; src/onelot/ui/index.html:407-465): The setup card, run bar,
  status lines, chart, tiles, log and a 'how it works' box are stacked in normal page flow. A
  chart of 460px with the panel beside it only from 1100px; on a laptop the chart bottom, the
  controls and the panel are not all in view. No sticky replay controls, no fill-to-height chart,
  no collapsed setup once running, no responsive dock for the panel.
- Page and chart are untied to the theme switch in app.js; fragile theming
  (src/onelot/ui/sim.js:662 (MutationObserver) and src/onelot/ui/app.js:72 (Charts.retheme)): The
  sim chart is not registered with charts.js's live map, so retheme works only through the page's
  own MutationObserver. Each cssv() call hits getComputedStyle; markers and lines are recreated on
  every retheme.
- No crosshair legend, last price line, volume pane or replay-oriented time axis
  (src/onelot/ui/sim.js:204-232): A replay or MetaTrader-style tester shows a live OHLC and time
  legend, the last price and a volume pane. Here the series has priceLineVisible false, no
  crosshair subscription, no volume, and the time axis is a +19800 shift with default tick labels.
  fitContent is the only view control, so the zoom jumps.
- Per-100 ms chart update and a per-minute network round trip at slow speeds; stale in-flight
  advances discarded but still block (src/onelot/ui/sim.js:427-466, :419-426): At 1x to 10x each
  minute needs a request that is not issued until the previous animation ends, so a slow server
  (the 390-minute lock hold, or a reload) produces a visible stall mid-replay with no prefetch
  buffer. S.busy blocks ticks while waiting, so the animation freezes. A page that fetched a few
  minutes ahead and animated locally would not freeze.
- refresh() pulls up to 300 candles from the server that the page ignores
  (src/onelot/ui/sim.js:378-383 and src/onelot/sim/routes.py:76-85): After every close, day end,
  take, skip and close the page calls GET /sessions/{id}, which builds candles_view and serialises
  about 300 rows that takeState never reads. Only the heal uses them. A lean state endpoint or a
  ?candles=0 switch would avoid it.
- Manager-wide RLock serialises every simulator call (src/onelot/sim/routes.py:87-95 and
  src/onelot/sim/manager.py:132): advance, take, skip, close, end and GET hold m.lock for the
  whole call, so a multi-second reload in one session blocks the others, including a Take click
  and the resume offer. A per-session lock would remove the cross-session stall.
- Setup changes silently decouple the controls from the running chart
  (src/onelot/ui/sim.js:624-634): Changing the instrument or timeframe select while a session runs
  changes the selects and the stored setup but not the chart, clock or panel. The page shows
  NIFTY's chart under a 'RELIANCE' selection until Start. There is no 'pending change' cue, no
  Apply, and no instant switch to a ready session.
- The animated minute path is invented, not market data (src/onelot/ui/sim.js:18, :69-74,
  :456-461): partialMinute draws open-low-high-close or open-high-low-close by candle colour, a
  plausible but fictional intrabar path between the real bar's four prices, at 100 ms steps. It
  never leaves the bar's high/low, so it is honest about the range, but a premium replay normally
  draws the real minute bars (a 1m timeframe) and this path should be labelled or replaced by an
  option such as 'tick path' off.
- sim.css is too small to carry a premium layout, and shared styles are used for important
  controls (src/onelot/ui/sim.css (57 lines) and src/onelot/ui/styles.css:179-199): Buttons,
  chips, selects and cards come from the shared sheet (skewed .btn with a literal-gradient take
  button at styles.css:189; chips as spans). No toolbar, segmented control, speed slider, dock,
  sticky header, drawer or tooltip rules. A rebuild needs its own layout system in sim.css (grid
  areas, container sizing).
- Position and alert are 'two cards that appear and disappear' rather than on-chart objects
  (src/onelot/ui/sim.js:128-165 and :236-256): Entry, stop and target are plain price lines. There
  is no risk/reward zone, no draggable-looking label with rupee amounts, no exit marker connected
  to the entry, and no on-chart alert banner. The owner asked to 'see stop loss and target on the
  chart' with an on-page notification; today the notification is a toast plus a card that may be
  below the fold (revealCard scrolls to it).
- Small page-side robustness gaps (src/onelot/ui/sim.js:45-46, :672-678, :604): The speed list
  comes from opts but the animation limit is 10x with a special 60x/300x path. Live mode hides
  speeds. loadTab on revisit only calls fitContent, which resets the user's zoom. A hidden tab
  pauses the replay; there is no resume cue. There is no error boundary: a thrown error in
  render() stops the clock silently.
