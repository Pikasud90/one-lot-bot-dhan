## What to build
1. Setup without a terminal: a "Telegram" card in the Settings tab.
   - Fields: bot token (a password field; after saving the page shows only "set", "not set"
     or "wrong shape", never the value) and chat id. Save writes `.env` the way the Dhan
     card does and puts the channel into use IN PLACE (no restart).
   - "Find my chat id": after the owner has sent any message to their bot, ask Telegram
     (`getUpdates`) with the saved token and list the chats found (id and the first name or
     title only); the owner picks one and it is saved.
   - "Send a test message": sends one and shows "sent" or the plain reason it failed.
   - Five numbered steps on the card in plain words: 1) in Telegram open @BotFather and send
     /newbot, copy the token it gives; 2) paste it here and save; 3) open your new bot and
     press Start; 4) Find my chat id; 5) Send a test message.
   - The token is a secret: never in a GET answer, a log line, an error text, the DOM after
     saving, the audit or a test fixture that looks real. Prove the redactor covers it.
2. What reaches the phone (write the table into the docs; one test per row):
   - every trade alert of the desk (SIGNAL): instrument, side, the contract, entry, stop,
     target, the line on how the trade is managed, valid until;
   - the "move your stop" notes of a taken position and the exit signal (EXIT), keeping the
     owner's rule "one exit per alert" (find where it is enforced; do not add a second exit
     message);
   - critical desk states as today (token expired, feed dead, halt);
   - NEW kind `token`: the reminders of point 3;
   - NEW kind `volume`: the Volume Watch (US-089 raises `Notification(level=WARNING,
     kind="volume", title=..., body=...)` through the Notifier; your part is only that the
     Telegram channel forwards WARNING notifications of kind `volume` and `token`, each with
     its own dedup key, and holds back nothing that is new).
   Find out what the channel forwards and holds back today (levels, rate limit, dedup,
   expiry of a queued alert) and write it down. Do not loosen a limit silently.
3. Token reminders: a pure decision function plus a step in a loop that already runs about
   once a minute (no new thread unless no loop can host it).
   - The expiry instant comes from `Engine.token_expiry()`. When it cannot be read, say so
     once ("cannot read when the Dhan token expires") and keep today's expired detection.
   - Stages: 60, 30, 15 and 5 minutes before the expiry, and "expired". Each fires once per
     token (key: expiry instant and stage), remembered in the database so a restart does not
     repeat it.
   - A start, or a first reading, inside a stage's window sends only the nearest stage not
     yet sent, with the true time left ("23 minutes left"), never a burst of older stages.
   - When the expiry instant moves forward: one message "Dhan token renewed: valid until
     21:31 on 25 Nov", and the ladder is armed again.
   - While expired: one reminder at 08:30 IST on each trading day ("renew before 09:15")
     until renewed. No other repeats.
   - Texts in plain words with the expiry time in IST and what to do ("Settings, Dhan card:
     Renew, or paste a new token"); when the desk can renew by itself
     (`secrets.can_self_renew`) the text says it will try. Never the token itself.
   - The same reminders go to the page (toast) and the computer through the existing
     channels, and the page shows the token's time left where it shows the token state today
     (find it: the header chip or the Today strip or the Settings card).
4. Status without secrets: the notifier's status (find the route; add `GET /api/notify/status`
   if none) says for Telegram: configured or not, last sent, last error class, queue length.
5. A test hook for development only: the Telegram API's base address may be overridden by
   the environment variable `TELEGRAM_API_BASE`, accepted ONLY when its host is 127.0.0.1 or
   localhost (anything else is ignored with one warning); never settable from the page.
6. Words: `docs/04_OPERATIONS.md` section "Telegram on the phone" (setup, what arrives, the
   reminder ladder, and what does NOT arrive: nothing is sent while the laptop sleeps, is
   offline or the desk is stopped); `.env.example` keys; a pointer in `QUICKSTART.md`.

## Acceptance criteria (tests under `tests/notify/` and `tests/api/` or where their kin live)
1. The ladder: a table-driven test of the decision function over a simulated clock: each
   stage once; a start at 23 minutes left sends one "23 minutes left" (stage 30) and later
   15, 5, expired; a renewal re-arms and announces; a restart (new object, same database)
   repeats nothing; an unreadable expiry is reported once; the 08:30 reminder only on
   trading days and only while expired.
2. End to end against a fake Telegram: a tiny local HTTP server (in the test) records
   requests; with `TELEGRAM_API_BASE` pointing at it: saving the token through the Settings
   route puts the channel into use; the test message arrives; a desk SIGNAL arrives with
   its fields; a `volume` WARNING and a `token` WARNING arrive; a repeated one does not.
3. Secrets: after saving, no GET route and no log record contains the token (search the
   captured logs and every JSON answer); a wrong-shaped token is refused with a plain
   sentence and is not written.
4. "Find my chat id" on recorded `getUpdates` shapes: private chat, group, no updates yet
   (plain sentence: "send any message to your bot first"), a 401 from Telegram ("the token
   was refused").
5. Nothing changes for a desk without Telegram keys: no error, no queue growth; the page's
   and the computer's notifications still work (existing tests untouched).
6. A private run you drive yourself: start this worktree's app in mock mode on port 8791
   with a fake Telegram on 127.0.0.1:8790, save a made-up token of the right shape through
   the Settings route, press test, wait for a mock alert, and show the fake's received
   texts in your report (they contain no secret). Stop both and remove `data` and `logs`.
