## Part 1: the pure indicator (`src/onelot/volume/indicator.py`, no I/O, numpy and pandas)
Functions for: turnover and up share of bars (three rules: `clv` = the contract's default;
`tick` = all of the minute's turnover to the side of close against open; `bvc` = the normal
distribution's share of the minute's return over the sd of the previous 60 one-minute
returns), expected turnover by minute of day, relative volume (1 minute, 5 minutes, day),
the spike score z, states, flow share, influence weights (non-negative least squares on
one-minute returns; scipy is installed) and pressure, alert decisions with cooldown, and
the day's profile (half hours, busiest, quietest). Everything takes frames or arrays and
returns frames or plain values; times are IST minute-of-day; a session with fewer than 300
bars is not "full" (Muhurat and half days). Tests in `tests/volume/` with hand-made bars
where every number can be checked by hand, plus a truncation test (cutting later bars never
changes an earlier value).

## Part 2: the study (sets the numbers; `scripts/volume_study.py`, run by RESEARCH-RULES 1)
Data: one-minute bars of the 18 desk stocks (volume since 2021; they end at 15:14 since the
closing auction began on 2026-08-03), of the indices (no volume) and of `INDIAVIX` in
`data/onelot.db` (`bars`: `series_key`, `resolution` '1m', `ts` naive UTC), read-only. Load
your indicator module by file path. Calibrate on TUNE, report TUNE / CHOOSE / TEST.
 V1. The day's shape: share of turnover by half hour, the busiest and quietest windows, by
     weekday, on NIFTY expiry days against other days.
 V2. Which up-share rule (`clv`, `tick`, `bvc`): the one whose 5-minute flow share agrees
     best with the index's own 5-minute return in the same window AND keeps the most
     information about the next 5 minutes; choose on TUNE; that rule becomes the product's.
 V3. Alert levels: the distribution of z; alerts a day at z of 2.5, 3.0, 3.5 for the market
     and 3.5, 4.0, 4.5 for single stocks with a 15-minute cooldown; choose the levels that
     give about 1 to 3 market alerts and at most about 6 stock alerts on an average day.
 V4. Size after a spike: the index's absolute return over the next 5, 15, 30 and 60 minutes
     after a market alert, over its median for the same minutes of the day on days without
     an alert there; per index (NIFTY, BANKNIFTY, FINNIFTY, SENSEX), with a day-clustered
     interval.
 V5. Direction after a spike: how often the index's next 5, 15, 30 minutes go the way of the
     spike's flow (BUYING up, SELLING down), the mean signed move in basis points with its t,
     and the same split by the move of the 5 minutes BEFORE the spike (does heavy volume
     after a fall mark the end of the fall?).
 V6. Pressure: does an index's `pressure_5m` tell its next 5 and 15 minutes? Rank correlation
     by day, and the spread between the top and bottom fifth in basis points, against the 5
     to 10 basis points an option buyer needs to cover costs.
 V7. Where it shows: the influence weights and `r2` per index; do spikes led by bank stocks
     move BANKNIFTY and FINNIFTY more than NIFTY?
 V8. India VIX: its change during and in the 30 minutes after a market spike; the day's
     relative volume against the day's VIX change; volume on high-VIX days.
 V9. The day ahead: does the first half hour's relative volume (09:15 to 09:44) tell the
     size of the rest of the day's range, and does its flow share tell the rest of the day's
     direction?
Say plainly for each: is it a size effect, a direction effect, or nothing; would it survive
an option buyer's costs. Write `docs/research/VOLUME_STUDY_2026-11-24.md` (at most 180
lines, RESEARCH-RULES section 8; if anything looks tradable add "Rows for the setup book")
and `src/onelot/volume/evidence.json` (the few facts of `/api/volume/study`, each with its
sentence, from this study's numbers; the route serves this file).
Workers: at most 2 processes.

## Part 3: the engine in the desk (`src/onelot/volume/`)
- `service.py`: builds the body of `/api/volume` for today from the bars the running desk
  already holds (find how the desk keeps each instrument's one-minute bars: `src/onelot/desk/
  desk.py`, `desk/data.py`, the trackers) and for a past date from the store (the app's own
  engine, read queries only). The baselines (expected turnover, z's mean and sd, influence
  weights) are built from the store once a session, off the desk's poll (a thread or a lazy
  cache with a lock), and rebuilt when the session date changes. A request never waits more
  than a moment: while the baselines build, the body says so in `notes`.
- A step in the desk's poll, about once a minute while the market is open: update, decide
  alerts (`volume_alerts` table: id primary key, so a restart does not repeat one), send
  each new alert through the Notifier as the contract says. The whole step sits in one
  guard: an exception is logged by class, counted, shown in `notes`, and the poll goes on.
- The order book (contract rule 2): one `quote` call a minute for the basket through the
  broker the desk already uses (`src/onelot/broker/dhan.py: quote` reads the best step; you
  need the five steps: extend its parsing without changing what today's callers get); store
  each reading in `volume_book` (keep 60 days); never during a broker error storm (back off
  after 3 failures in a row until the next session). In mock mode the mock broker's quotes.
- Config: block `volume:` in `config/desk.yaml` and its typed reader in `src/onelot/desk/
  config.py` (basket, z_alert, stock_z_alert, cooldown_min, alerts_on, notify, book,
  max_alerts_per_day default 12). Tables in `src/onelot/store/schema.py`.
- `routes.py`: `register(app, ...)` with the four routes; ONE import and ONE call in
  `src/onelot/api/server.py` (the way `src/onelot/sim/routes.py` is registered).
- Speed: the minute step under 200 ms on the 18-stock basket; `/api/volume` for today under
  300 ms once the baselines exist; a past date under 3 s the first time, cached after.
- A tool for the lead: `scripts/volume_replay.py --store PATH --date YYYY-MM-DD` opens the
  store read-only, runs the service for that date and prints the day's headline, alerts and
  top stocks, and checks them against a direct computation with `indicator.py`.

## Acceptance criteria (tests under `tests/volume/`)
1. Every formula of the contract on hand-made bars; the three up-share rules; a stock with
   no bars goes to `basket.missing` and the rest still answers.
2. No look-ahead (truncation) in expected turnover, z, influence and alerts.
3. An alert is raised once, respects the cooldown and the daily cap, survives a restart
   (same database: no repeat), and reaches a recording Notifier with level WARNING and kind
   `volume`; with `alerts_on: false` nothing is sent; with `notify: false` it is stored and
   shown but not sent.
4. The desk's poll survives an exception thrown inside the volume step (one test through the
   real poll method, not a fake).
5. The four routes answer with the contract's keys in mock mode (FastAPI test client), a bad
   date is a 400 with a plain sentence, a past date answers from stored bars.
6. Half days and the 15:14 end: a session of 60 bars is not used for baselines; a baseline
   minute with no history gives `expected` 0 and an rvol of null, never a division error.
7. The order book parser on a recorded-shape fixture (five steps each side); a broker error
   leaves `book` null and the poll alive.
8. `scripts/volume_replay.py` runs on a small fixture store in a test.
